## Reproduce

```bash
# 1. choose the adaptive engine's blend on 2015 (data/raw/validation comes from rebuild.py)
python pipelines/evaluate.py --ml32m data/raw/ml-32m --art data/raw/validation \
  --cutoff 2015-01-01 --until 2016-01-01 --users 3000 --seed 42 --no-ope --label validation \
  --blend-grid docs/benchmarks/hybrid_grid.json --out docs/benchmarks/validation.json
# 2. the test period, before and after, on the same people
python pipelines/evaluate.py --ml32m data/raw/ml-32m --art data/raw/baseline-artifacts \
  --users 3000 --seed 42 --no-ope --label before --save-users docs/benchmarks/users.json \
  --out docs/benchmarks/before.json --per-user docs/benchmarks/before.per_user.json.gz
python pipelines/evaluate.py --ml32m data/raw/ml-32m --art data/artifacts \
  --universe data/raw/baseline-artifacts/catalog.json --users-file docs/benchmarks/users.json \
  --no-ope --label after-common --out docs/benchmarks/after-common.json \
  --per-user docs/benchmarks/after-common.per_user.json.gz
python pipelines/evaluate.py --ml32m data/raw/ml-32m --art data/artifacts \
  --users-file docs/benchmarks/users.json --label after-full --out docs/benchmarks/after-full.json \
  --per-user docs/benchmarks/after-full.per_user.json.gz
cp docs/benchmarks/after-full.json data/artifacts/eval.json        # what /api/evaluation serves
python pipelines/benchmark_report.py
```

`data/raw/baseline-artifacts` is `data/artifacts` as it was deployed before the expansion (git commit in its `COMMIT` file).

## Two-tower model selection

Chosen on validation only — 2015 likes from earlier likes; model trained on likes before 2015. Films first liked in 2015 are scored by content alone, as a new film is served. ID dropout trains the model to score a film from its content on the same scale as one it knows; without it, films with no training data crowd the lists.

## Method

- **Split.** Temporal, at 2016-01-01, on MovieLens 32M. Ratings before it are the only training data for every model — item-item CF, popularity and the quality prior are rebuilt from them here, and the two-tower model was trained on them, with its epoch chosen on a validation set inside the training period. Ratings after it are the test set and are never used for selection.
- **People.** Drawn once (seed 42) from those with ≥ 10 positives (≥ 4★) before the cutoff and ≥ 3 positives after it on films they hadn't rated; the same people are used in every run (`docs/benchmarks/users.json`). Their training ratings are the input, with their real timestamps.
- **Targets.** A later ≥ 4★ rating on a film the person hadn't rated is a hit. Films already rated are never recommended.
- **Common universe.** `after-common` restricts both candidates and targets to the films of the old catalogue, so it has exactly the test set of `before` and the two can be compared person by person. `after-full` scores the whole expanded catalogue; its test set is larger, so its accuracy is *not* comparable with `before`.
- **Metrics.** Recall@K = hits in the top K / min(|targets|, K). Precision@K = hits / K. NDCG@K with binary gains. MAP@20 = mean average precision, normalised by min(|targets|, 20). Coverage = distinct films recommended / candidate films. Diversity = mean pairwise (1 − cosine) of the films' content vectors within a list. Novelty = mean −log2 of a film's share of training users who liked it. Cold-start share = recommended films with no training ratings. Cold / warm recall = Recall@20 on the targets without / with training ratings. Latency = time to rank one person's list in this harness (single thread, warm), not HTTP latency.
- **Intervals.** 95% percentile bootstrap over people (1,000 resamples within a run, 2,000 for the paired before/after differences).

## What these numbers cannot tell you

- A held-out positive (≥4★ after the cutoff) is a success; a film not rated afterwards counts as a miss, though the person may never have seen it. MovieLens ratings are missing not at random.
- The logging policy is simulated and its propensities are known exactly. A live system must log them — CINEMIND records the probability of every exploration slot it shows.
- Each logged event is one film shown to one user; slates and position effects are not modelled.
- Taste is assumed stable across the cutoff, apart from what the drift model captures.
- These are relative comparisons between methods on the same data, not predictions of live click-through.
- Offline recall only rewards films a person went on to rate, so it can measure what exploration costs but not what it learns. That is why exploration slots log their propensities: the counterfactual estimators need them once real feedback exists.
- The single temporal split tests drift only loosely: the test period runs to 2023, and recency is measured from each person's last rating before 2016, not from the moment they would see the list.
- The adaptive engine runs here exactly as deployed, except that its co-rating graph is item-item CF rebuilt from training data: the production graph was built from all 32M ratings and would leak the test period.
- Film descriptions use viewer tags collected up to 2023, a small content-side leak into the test period.
- The before/after difference is the change of the whole system: a retrained two-tower model, new content vectors for added films, and — because the catalogue now holds more of the films people rated — longer training histories for some of them. It does not isolate any one of these.
- Popularity, item-item CF and the quality prior see only films with training ratings, so films added without ratings can only be reached through content: the two-tower model's content projection and the sentence embeddings.
